# The codex

The deck draws a closed vocabulary of signs. Every sign here is registered in
`src/design/codex.rs`, and a test holds the two together: a sign the code can
draw has a heading in this note, and no two signs share a name or a meaning.
This note is generated from the registry's tables; edit the tables.

Three hands touched the machine, and each sign carries what it meant to each:

- **Machine.** The deck is alien circuitry: traces that run orthogonally and
  bend at forty-five degrees, ending in pads; vias where lines cross;
  octagonal shields. This is what the sign *is*, electrically.
- **Talisman.** The old humans who received the deck read its traces as a
  Daoist register. Tracks are generals, one per column; clips are registers,
  one per row; devices are seals; the transport paces the Dipper; the master
  is where every bus arrives. This is what the sign was *to them*.
- **Annotation.** A later, Rosicrucian hand wrote in the margins in a
  typewriter voice, with arrows. This is what the sign is to someone working
  the deck from notes: the plain meaning.

Strokes are written on a seven-by-seven lattice, `x,y` from the top-left,
segments separated by `|`. A segment with a single point is a pad. Every move
is orthogonal or at forty-five degrees, because that is how the board bends.
